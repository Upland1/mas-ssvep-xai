# Trial-Grouped, Continuously Filtered Evaluation of a Five-Class SSVEP Decoder: Leakage Correction, Connectivity Statistics, Detection and Leave-One-Subject-Out

*Technical report v2 of the mas-ssvep-eeg-xai rebuild (phase P24). It supersedes the historical report v1 (`docs/report/v1/report.md`, P06), whose "leakage-free" claim was wrong. Every number is copied from a CSV or log under `reports/` (git-ignored, regenerated by `make all`) or from a phase record that quotes one; the path is given with the number. Reference values of the original project are labelled R-x and are targets for diagnosis, not results of this work.*

## Abstract

We rebuilt a five-class steady-state visually evoked potential (SSVEP) decoder for eight parieto-occipital channels recorded at 256 Hz and reproduced the original project's history in gated phases. The legacy protocol used window-level cross-validation, which places windows of one trial in both training and test folds. With trial-grouped cross-validation the classical flagship (FBCSP and FBCCA features, scaler, Shrinkage-LDA) falls from 83.33 % to 79.28 % at 1.0 s on five subjects (S01–S05), a classical inflation of 4.06 percentage points (pp) (`reports/cv_leakage_demo.csv`). The corrected continuous build (recorded 256 Hz, continuous filtering before windowing, eight-channel montage, true trial identifiers, one shared quality gate) gives 87.38 % at 1.0 s (`reports/v1_vs_v2/flagship.csv`); a narrow harmonic filter bank raises the five-class result to 94.29 % (`reports/n_subjects_benchmark_p17_harm_5class.csv`). Leave-one-subject-out (LOSO) Shrinkage-LDA reaches 87.61 % and a within-subject reference 90.79 % on the same channels (`reports/loso_w1_p22_common/summary.csv`). The P23 connectivity-edge graph neural network (GNN), a three-seed ensemble, gives 89.22 % under LOSO and 86.81 % within subject (`reports/loso_gnn/summary.csv`, `reports/within_gnn/summary.csv`). Several results do not replicate the original project's numbers: the sequential forward selection row, one Compact-CNN cell (S03, 2.0 s, augmentation ON) and one legacy GNN cell (S02, 2.0 s). We report them as documented deviations. The cohort is five subjects; the findings are descriptive for that cohort and do not establish performance on new participants.

## 1. Introduction

SSVEP brain–computer interfaces decode the frequency of a flickering target from scalp EEG. The paradigm has five targets at 24.00, 20.00, 15.00, 10.9091 and 8.5714 Hz, shown in 5-second segments with a fixation cross (code 201) and a cue (code 202) around each trial [1, 2]. The original project of this rebuild built a classical pipeline and a series of neural and graph models on these recordings. Its published numbers include a leak-free flagship, an augmentation A/B for three networks, a connectivity gate, a connectivity statistics study and a leave-one-subject-out study [3]. The rebuild replays that history in 26 phases, reproducing legacy behaviour where the roadmap requires it and fixing each defect with a before/after comparison [4].

This report has three aims. First, to correct the evaluation: to measure the size of the trial leak and to report corrected numbers. Second, to document the Era-C changes (sampling rate, continuous filtering, valid windows, a sixth rest class, harmonic and intermediate bands, graph-level connectivity statistics, window length and detection, LOSO and a GNN under LOSO). Third, to report every deviation from the original numbers rather than tuning toward them [4].

## 2. Materials and Methods

### 2.1 Data source

The recordings are the `OO` (on-off flicker) calibration session of five participants, S01–S05, and the `mOO` run used as a pseudo-online test set. Of the 23 recordings in the measured inventory, 10 were delivered (OO and mOO of S01–S05); the 13 others are absent and no phase uses them [2, 5]. Each run has 40 trials of 8 per target in randomized order, and the recordings are sampled at 256 Hz [2, 6]. Channels are `PO7, PO3, POz, PO4, PO8, O1, Oz, O2`; PO7 is excluded in the legacy configuration and restored in P07 [6, 7]. The P21 LOSO study also ran on a 39-subject cohort (S01–S040 without S020) as a separate data source; this report uses the 5-subject cohort for every main result [14].

### 2.2 Legacy and corrected protocols

Legacy behaviour is reproduced and labelled. Its defects are the window-level cross-validation (PF-01), the `i % 5` smoothing that is misaligned after a rejected window (PF-02), the assumed 250 Hz sampling rate (PF-04), per-window sub-band filtering (PF-14) and a per-script quality gate (R5, legacy by design) [4, 8]. The corrected protocol (from P16) uses trial-grouped cross-validation, `StratifiedGroupKFold(5, shuffle, 42)` on trial identifiers (R1), continuous filtering before cutting (R3), fs from the recording metadata and one shared quality decision with explicit thresholds (R5) [4].

Trial identifiers on legacy folders are reconstructed from pre-gate labels and indexed with the gate's mask (PF-03). Approximate identifiers remain on S02 and S04 (39 of 199 and 38 of 198 stimulus windows are in inconsistent legacy groups); the corrected continuous build has true identifiers [5, 7].

### 2.3 Features and classifiers

FBCSP uses five sub-bands, (7, 12), (13, 17), (18, 26), (27, 36) and (38, 52) Hz, with one one-vs-rest CSP component per side (m = 1), fitted on the training rows of each fold [5]. FBCCA gives five canonical-correlation scores with three harmonics. The flagship classifier is a `StandardScaler` followed by Shrinkage-LDA [5, 9]. Feature selection (ANOVA, mutual information, sequential forward selection and sub-band pruning) is in-fold [5]. The continuous-build banks of P17 are `base` (the five FBCSP sub-bands), `harm` (base plus k·f bands, k = 1..3) and `harm+mid` (plus midpoints), each ±1 Hz and order 2 [7, 8].

### 2.4 Networks, augmentation and the training convention

Three legacy networks and three networks of the detection and LOSO phases are used. EEGNet (2,349 parameters at 7 × 256), Compact-CNN (`compact_cnn`, 56,021) and ShallowConvNet (`shallow_convnet`, 14,485) are the legacy set [6]. `compact_eegnet` and `eegnet` (kernel 128) and `ccnn` are the P20–P21 networks [10, 12]. `ccnn` has 27,977 parameters at 7 channels and 32,309 at 8 (`reports/nn_sweep/summary.csv`, column `Params`) after the P25 layout fix. The naming is kept distinct (PF-31): `compact_cnn` is the `CompactCNN` class and is not `compact_eegnet`. Training uses per-window z-scoring, AdamW (weight decay 1e-2), cosine annealing, label smoothing 0.05 and seed 42 + fold. In the P20–P23 protocol the validation split is 20 % of training trials, patience 25 and at most 150 epochs. Augmentation (Gaussian noise, amplitude scaling, time shift and intra-class mixup, each with probability 0.5) is applied to the training fold only [6]. Training accuracy is measured on the un-augmented training fold (PF-30).

**CCNN spec deviation, fixed in P25 (fix round 1, code in 987742b).** From P20 to P24 the CCNN stacked [real, imag] as two input maps with bias-free convolutions (pre-P25 layout, non-conforming: 14,817 / 17,285 parameters). The roadmap and R-8 require one `[real | imag] / T` map with convolution biases (27,977 / 32,309 parameters). Every CCNN value produced before P25 is non-conforming and superseded; the CCNN values in this document are the P25 regenerated outputs.

### 2.5 Connectivity and graph statistics

Coherence is estimated once per condition from concatenated windows (R4), not averaged per window, because the per-window average is biased upward (PF-15). The baseline is the matched 201 windows in the stimulus band (PF-16). Phase connectivity uses the cross-spectral density (R3); PDC uses a strided MVAR fit (PF-17, R6) [4, 9]. Statistics are trial-level permutations with the observed group sizes (R7), Benjamini–Hochberg over edges (FDR) and the Network-Based Statistic (NBS), reported as separate criteria [9, 11]. Graph models use electrode nodes with power and FBCCA features and optional coherence edges [10].

### 2.6 Detection and LOSO

Window length is swept from 0.5 to 4 s (stride 0.25 s) for FBCCA, in-fold LDA and a network, with accuracy and window-level information transfer rate (ITR, bits/min with N = 5) [12]. A pseudo-online detector is trained on OO and replayed causally on mOO [12]. LOSO trains on every subject but one (the group is the subject, R1); the within-subject reference uses `StratifiedGroupKFold(5)` on trial identifiers of the same subject [11]. The LOSO lda_subjnorm rows normalize each held-out session with its own unlabelled whole-session statistics. These are test-time normalization results and an offline upper bound: the held-out session's unlabelled statistics are used, and no labels are used (R2 exception). A first-K warm-up study (K trials, unlabelled) is reported separately [11, 14].

### 2.7 Reproducibility

Each phase ran its commands from a clean checkout, and the verifier reproduced the reported CSVs. The full test suite runs on CPU without recordings (1513 tests passed at P23) [14]. All CSVs are regenerated by `make all`; the per-phase targets are listed in the README.

The step from 79.28 % to 87.38 % at 1.0 s is a decomposition with several parts (`reports/v1_vs_v2/flagship.csv`). The sampling-rate correction (fs 250 to 256 Hz in the features) contributes +8.04 pp at 1.0 s (+6.91 pp at 2.0 s) and is positive in every subject, largest where the legacy accuracy is lowest (S02 +18.47 pp). Continuous filtering is therefore the correct signal path, with no accuracy effect here; the guard is kept for signal validity rather than accuracy.

### 3.2 Edge effects and filter bank

Edge effects of per-window sub-band filtering on S03 (`reports/edge_effects_summary.csv`): the edge-to-centre error ratio is 8.17 for 7–12 Hz, 7.45 for 13–17 Hz and 21.00 for 18–26 Hz, with edge errors of 53.24 %, 56.68 % and 33.10 % against centre errors of 6.51 %, 7.60 % and 1.58 %. The pre-committed criterion (ratio at least 5 and edge error at least 20 %) is met. The order-of-magnitude reading (ratio at least 10) is not met for the two lowest bands, and we say so.

### 3.3 Six classes and the rest class

The like-for-like check is the stimulus correct rate of the six-class model against the five-class smoothed accuracy: StimCorrect(6) is below Smoothed(5) in 15 of 15 cells (`reports/n_subjects_benchmark_p17_*_6class.csv`). The narrow banks mainly reduce false activations on S01 and S02 (FalseAct at 2.0 s from 22.00 to 7.12 % and from 16.08 to 3.24 %), while Missed rises on S01 (14.25 to 20.00 %).

### 3.4 Connectivity statistics

Connectivity was analysed as a cohort with trial-level permutations (200 permutations for the P13 comparison, 1000 for P18). The FDR totals per subject of the P13 final run are 0 / 0 / 64 / 49 / 12 for coherence and 4 / 0 / 42 / 28 / 5 for wPLI (S01–S05; `reports/connectivity_cohort_summary.csv`), against the original's 0 / 0 / 65 / 51 / 15 and 0 / 3 / 38 / 33 / 18 (R-12). The S05 wPLI total of five is an accepted deviation: the same procedure with other seed bases gives 7 to 14, and the reported value is not changed [9].

The pairwise exchange null is off-centre on real data, so its p-values are reported as exchangeability tests with a centred diagnostic beside them [9, 13].

### 3.5 Connectivity gate and graph neural network (within subject)

The connectivity gate asks whether edge information adds to node power and FBCCA features. Shrinkage-LDA at 1.0 s with five repeats gives Base (75 features) 73.75 %, Base + Ec 77.57 % and Ec alone 48.59 % (cohort means of subject means computed for this report from `reports/connectivity_gate_benchmark.csv`, `k_select = 0`). The gate does not pass: none of the three responders gains at least 2 pp (S03 +1.22, S04 +0.10, S05 −1.70 pp); S01 (+16.30) and S02 (+3.16) gain. The reference cohort result is R-14: 77.6 % for Base + Ec [3].

Two GNN results must be kept apart. The legacy GNN of P15 (legacy folders, fs 250, three repeats, CPU) is the one compared with the original's R-15: on S02, S03 and S05 the reference is reproduced within tolerance on 11 of 12 subject cells, and one cell is not, `nodes+F` on S02 at 2.0 s, 37.07 % against the original's 46.1 % (all three repeats fall below; `reports/gnn_benchmark.csv`). Sebastian accepted this as a documented deviation (B007; cause not established) [10, 14]. Its cohort mean at 1.0 s for `nodes+F` over S01–S05 is 64.33 % (computed for this report from the same CSV). Train accuracy of the legacy GNN is above the original's on all graded cells; the definition is the same, so the cause (device or random-number stream) is open.

The P23 within-subject GNN (continuous build, seven common channels, 1.0 s, three-seed ensemble, `reports/within_gnn/summary.csv`) has no R-15 target; it is compared only with the P23 LOSO rows and the classical references. Its cohort means are 86.81 % (`nodes+F`) and 86.98 % (`nodes+coh+F`). Per seed, the `nodes+F` cohort means are 85.42, 85.21 and 86.01 % (seeds 42, 43, 44; `reports/within_gnn/seeds.csv`).

## 4. Discussion

**The leak and its size.** Window-level cross-validation inflates the classical flagship by 4.06 pp at 1.0 s and 4.96 pp at 2.0 s on five subjects. The inflation is reproduced on every subject and is largest on S02 (−10.21 pp), where approximate trial identifiers survive [5]. The network inflation in the original is not reproduced here. Like for like, the three-base EEGNet cohort drop is −2.71 pp (OFF) and −2.82 pp (ON), against the seed-42 cohort drop of −5.57 pp; on S03 the seed-42 drop of −20.81 pp is of the same order as the three-base training-seed range of 16.75 pp (`mas/state/reports/P11.md`). 13 of 14 out-of-band augmentation cells are explained by seed variance; the one that is not (Compact-CNN S03 2.0 s ON: 129, 127 and 129 of 197 windows over three seeds, against a smallest in-band count of 131) is a documented deviation [5, 6, 14]. The leaky numbers are therefore not a basis for claims about networks.

**Augmentation.** Leak-free augmentation helps all three networks on average, most for Compact-CNN (+13.56 pp at 1.0 s, against the original's +17.16 pp). The gain is not a ranking of networks: the cohort means are on five subjects, and the legacy networks have one seed set in the A/B (Table 8; the per-subject seed ranges are in the P11 record) [6].

**Continuous filtering and sampling rate.** The largest corrected change is the sampling rate, not the filter. Continuous filtering improves the signal path (no window-edge effects) but does not change accuracy here, which is the expected result for a filter that acts on signal validity rather than on class information. The narrow harmonic bank carries the gain of the five-class result: its improvement is consistent with an SNR gain in the ±1 Hz bands at the harmonic frequencies, but the bank contains the fundamentals and the ablation (fundamentals only) is still open.

**Connectivity.** Connectivity statistics agree with the original's pattern at 24 Hz (significant in S03 to S05 for coherence and wPLI; S01 and S02 null) and differ in exact counts. The S05 wPLI total and the NBS and FDR cells that flip between permutation counts are inconclusive at five subjects, and the alpha-band prediction holds for coherence and PLV only [9, 13]. Graph sizes are large for S03 and S04 and small for S01 and S02, which suggests that graph-level statistics follow subject-level SNR rather than a frequency effect; this is a hypothesis on five subjects.

**Confusions.** Table 4 shows that most stimulus errors of the six-class model go to the rest class, which is consistent with the rest class absorbing weak responses. The low-frequency pair (10.91 and 8.57 Hz) is the most confused among the stimulus classes, as R-3 reports for the legacy model.

**Generalization.** Test accuracy is far below train accuracy on S01 and S02 for the legacy networks and for the LOSO GNN (gaps of 13.8 to 27.8 pp). For EEGNet with augmentation off, the legacy leaky values are 28.00 and 27.04 % test against 83.25 and 81.38 % train (`reports/legacy_leaky/augmentation_ab_eegnet.csv`, P10); the grouped values are 30.50 and 26.02 % test against 82.99 and 79.60 % train (`reports/augmentation_ab_eegnet.csv`, Table 8). This is a generalization failure, not an optimization failure. On S03 to S05 the LOSO test accuracy is above train accuracy by 1.4 to 5.6 pp. For lda the LOSO cohort mean is below the matched within-subject reference (§3.6); for the GNN the reference direction is not observed [11, 13].

**Limitations.** The cohort has five subjects (39 for LOSO only in the classical study), one OO session per subject for the main results, and one or three training seeds for the networks. Several differences are a few windows; one window is 0.51 pp at 197 windows. The sequential forward selection row (ours 89.85 / 95.94, R-4 84.77 / 93.40) is ours, not a reproduction. Within-subject GNN training reached the 150-epoch cap in 19 of 50 folds, a stated limitation. The trial-level permutation statistics are reported with their resolution limits [5, 9, 10].

## 5. Conclusion

Trial-grouped cross-validation corrects the legacy leak: the classical flagship is 79.28 % at 1.0 s and 82.61 % at 2.0 s on five subjects, against 83.33 % and 87.57 % for the leaky protocol (`reports/n_subjects_benchmark.csv`). The corrected continuous build reaches 87.38 % (`reports/v1_vs_v2/flagship.csv`), and a narrow harmonic bank 94.29 % (`reports/n_subjects_benchmark_p17_harm_5class.csv`). Under LOSO, Shrinkage-LDA reaches 87.61 % and the within-subject reference 90.79 % on the common channels; the connectivity-edge GNN, as a three-seed ensemble, reaches 89.22 % under LOSO and 86.81 % within subject. Each result is for five subjects, and the deviations of §3 and §4 are reported rather than removed. The next steps are the open items of the progress report: the sequential forward selection configuration, the smoothing rule on legacy folders, the cause of the single unreproduced cells, and a second cohort.
